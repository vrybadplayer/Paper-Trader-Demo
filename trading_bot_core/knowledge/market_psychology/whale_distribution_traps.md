# Whale Distribution Traps

## Definition
Whale distribution traps occur when large holders (whales) of an asset systematically offload their positions to retail investors, often under the guise of bullish news or false breakouts, leading to a sharp price decline after the retail FOMO peak.

## Characteristics
- Gradual price increase on low volume, masking distribution.
- Sudden spike in volume and price on news, attracting retail buying.
- Whales sell into the strength, causing the price to stall or reverse.
- Often followed by a rapid decline as buying interest dries up.

## Indicators
- High volume during price increases but weak follow-through buying.
- Divergence between price and volume (price up, volume down on up days).
- Large sell orders appearing in the order book during rallies.
- Increased volatility and wide bid-ask spreads during distribution phases.
- Options activity showing unusually high put buying or call selling.

## Typical Patterns
1. **Accumulation**: Whales quietly accumulate over time.
2. **Markup**: Price rises gradually, often with low volume, to attract attention.
3. **Distribution**: Whales distribute to retail on news or breakouts, volume increases but price struggles to make new highs.
4. **Markdown**: Price collapses as whales have exited and retail is left holding.

## Risk Considerations
- Distribution traps can be mistaken for genuine breakouts.
- Entering on high volume breakouts without confirmation can lead to being caught in the trap.
- Watch for signs of weakening momentum during upward moves.

## Mitigation Strategies
- Use volume analysis to confirm breakouts (volume should increase on up days and decrease on down days during healthy trends).
- Look for signs of distribution (e.g., high volume closes near the low of the day).
- Consider waiting for a pullback or confirmation before entering on breakouts.
- Monitor whale wallet activity (if available) for large transfers to exchanges.
