# Liquidity Sweeps and Manipulation

## Definition
Liquidity sweeps (or stop hunts) are rapid price moves designed to trigger stop-loss orders and liquidity pools, often orchestrated by larger participants to fill their own orders at favorable prices before reversing the price direction.

## Characteristics
- Sharp, short-lived price spikes beyond recent highs or lows.
- Occurs during low liquidity periods (e.g., overnight, lunch time, before news).
- Price quickly reverses after sweeping the liquidity pool.
- Often accompanied by high volume on the spike but low follow-through.

## Indicators
- Price breaks a recent swing high or low with increased volume, then rapidly reverses.
- Clusters of stop-loss orders just beyond technical levels (e.g., above resistance, below support).
- Presence of large market orders that absorb liquidity and cause the spike.
- Time of day: common during Asian session, lunch break, or pre-market/post-market hours.
- Order book shows large iceberg orders or hidden liquidity on the opposite side of the sweep.

## Typical Patterns
1. **Accumulation/Distribution**: Large player builds a position quietly.
2. **Liquidity Sweep**: Price moves to trigger stops and liquidity, allowing the large player to fill opposite orders.
3. **Reversal**: Price moves back in the opposite direction as the large player has achieved their objective.
4. **Continuation or Consolidation**: Depending on the intent, price may continue the original trend or consolidate.

## Risk Considerations
- Sweeps can trigger premature stop-outs for retail traders.
- False breakouts based on sweeps can lead to losing trades if not recognized.
- Requires awareness of market structure and liquidity pools.

## Mitigation Strategies
- Use wider stop-loss placements or consider volatility-based stops.
- Avoid placing stops exactly at obvious levels (e.g., round numbers, recent highs/lows).
- Look for confirmation of reversal after a sweep (e.g., price action, volume, order flow).
- Consider using options strategies that are less sensitive to sudden spikes (e.g., vertical spreads).
- Monitor time and sales for large block trades that may indicate sweeping intent.
